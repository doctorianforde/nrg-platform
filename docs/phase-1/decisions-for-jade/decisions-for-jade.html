A. New this week: student questions and what students see

Your Template 3 is now built on the test site. A student gets an assignment the platform picks (topic, domain, taxonomy level, stem length), writes the stem, four options and an optional rationale, and an AI grader scores it — Stem /30, Options /45, fit to the assignment /25, rationale bonus up to +5, 75 to pass — with warm, specific feedback on each bar. These choices decide how it runs.

D1How should student questions be graded?

Both ways are built and use the same rubric and feedback. The difference is cost and speed.

  • Instant, through the paid AI service. Feedback within a minute of submitting. Costs roughly 3–5 US cents per question (about US$30–50 per 1,000 questions). Needs an API account with a card on file.
  • Queued, on the AI subscriptions we already pay for. No cost per question. Questions wait in a queue and are graded in batches when Ian (or an assistant) runs the grader — typically the same day. Students see "waiting for grading" until then.
  • Start queued, switch to instant once paying students make same-minute feedback worth the cost. Switching is one setting; nothing else changes.
  • No AI grading — teachers review student questions as before.
Our recommendation: option 3. If the instant service is ever down, questions fall back to the queue automatically, so none goes ungraded.

D2What does an AI "pass" mean?

Your template says a question scoring 75+ "enters the student question bank". Today a teacher still approves every question before students can see it, and XP is paid on that approval.

  • The grade is advice. A teacher approves every question; the AI grade and feedback sit beside it on the review page.
  • A pass goes straight into a separate "student questions" bank that students can practise from, labelled as student-written; teachers spot-check later.
Our recommendation: advice only for now. The grader is good, but a wrong answer key reaching students costs more trust than a teacher's minute. The grader never passes a question whose key it believes is wrong.
